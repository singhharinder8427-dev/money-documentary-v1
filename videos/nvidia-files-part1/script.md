## CHAPTER 1 - Out of Nowhere
- **NARRATOR:** Rewind to 2023, in Singapore.
- **NARRATOR:** A little-known company called Megaspeed has just been reborn.
- **NARRATOR:** Before that, it was the Singapore arm of a Chinese video game company.
- **NARRATOR:** Within days of its sale to a Singapore company, reports say, it became an official Nvidia cloud partner.
- **NARRATOR:** Now it wanted to rent out computing power. The kind built on Nvidia chips.
- **NARRATOR:** Everyone wants those chips. Not everyone is allowed to have them.
- **NARRATOR:** Jensen Huang co-founded Nvidia in 1993. For thirty-three years, he was its only CEO.
- **NARRATOR:** Its boss was Alice Huang. A Chinese national, according to United States reports. A Hong Kong resident, according to Singapore.
- **NARRATOR:** No relation to Jensen Huang. Same last name. That is all.
- **NARRATOR:** In 2023, Megaspeed's assets were about thirty-three million dollars. [COUNTER 0->33000000 gold]
- **NARRATOR:** A year later, almost three billion. [COUNTER 33000000->3000000000 gold]
- **ANALYST:** Thirty-three million to three billion? In one year? (dramatised)
- **INVESTOR:** Maybe it was a very good year. (dramatised)
- **ANALYST:** Maybe. (dramatised)
- **NARRATOR:** One Tufts professor said the company appeared out of nowhere.
- **NARRATOR:** Then, on December seventh, 2023, a photo. Jensen and Alice Huang, at a Singapore restaurant.
- **NARRATOR:** By September 2024, a United States official was walking through a data center in Malaysia.
- **INSPECTOR:** Why are these Nvidia servers still sitting in their boxes? (dramatised)
- **COLLEAGUE:** Should I take photos? (dramatised)
- **INSPECTOR:** Of every box. (dramatised)
- **NARRATOR:** Remember those boxes. Soon, Washington will start asking about them. [CARD]

## CHAPTER 2 - Still in the Boxes
- **NARRATOR:** So what was Washington worried about? Chips.
- **NARRATOR:** Since October 2022, the most advanced Nvidia AI chips are banned from China.
- **NARRATOR:** The Justice Department says smugglers keep trying. Hundreds of millions of dollars' worth, over several years.
- **NARRATOR:** Singapore noticed Megaspeed too. In February 2025, its police and customs opened an investigation.
- **NARRATOR:** The suspicion: false declarations about server purchases. Singapore says it acted on its own. No foreign request.
- **NARRATOR:** Then came June twenty-fourth, 2025.
- **NARRATOR:** United States export officials inspect a Megaspeed site. Alice Huang is there.
- **NARRATOR:** That same day, Singapore police interview her. And impound her passport.
- **OFFICER:** Please hand over your passport. You are not leaving yet. (dramatised)
- **NARRATOR:** The same month, Nvidia says it cut Megaspeed off. No new orders. No shipments.
- **CLERK:** New order from Megaspeed. (dramatised)
- **MANAGER:** Not anymore. (dramatised)
- **NARRATOR:** It has not said why. [CARD]
- **NARRATOR:** Two months later, on August thirtieth, Alice Huang leaves Megaspeed. That is the company's account.
- **NARRATOR:** Hold that date.
- **NARRATOR:** A short seller called Culper Research quoted an employee who said she was still the boss.
- **NARRATOR:** The records and the claims point in different directions. [CARD]
- **NARRATOR:** In October 2025, the New York Times asked it bluntly. Is China buying banned Nvidia chips? It said Megaspeed was poised to buy two billion dollars of Nvidia technology within a year.
- **NARRATOR:** But the biggest question was never about chips. It was about who she was talking to.

## CHAPTER 3 - How the Door Opens
- **NARRATOR:** So how do banned chips allegedly reach China? Three ways.
- **NARRATOR:** One. Smuggling. Prosecutors in a separate case say workers swapped labels with a hair dryer.
- **WORKER:** This sticker will not budge. (dramatised)
- **COLLEAGUE:** Use the hair dryer. (dramatised)
- **NARRATOR:** Two. Middlemen. A company in Southeast Asia buys the servers and quietly passes them on.
- **NARRATOR:** Three. Renting. The chips never leave Asia. A buyer in China just logs in from afar.
- **NARRATOR:** In May 2025, the Commerce Department warned United States firms to watch for partners offering cloud access to China.
- **NARRATOR:** Think of a pizza oven banned in one town. Nobody can sell you the oven. But anyone can sell you pizza.
- **CUSTOMER:** One pizza oven, please. (dramatised)
- **SELLER:** Sorry. Ovens are banned in this town. (dramatised)
- **CUSTOMER:** Then I'll take a pizza. (dramatised)
- **SELLER:** Pizza is fine. (dramatised)
- **NARRATOR:** No one has proven Megaspeed did any of this. The company flatly denies it. [CARD]
- **NARRATOR:** Last December it said there was no evidence of illicit chip transfers.
- **NARRATOR:** But United States intelligence wasn't only watching the chips. It was listening.

- **NARRATOR:** Here is what almost nobody outside Washington knew.

## CHAPTER 4 - Completely Normal
- **NARRATOR:** October twenty-ninth. The Times goes public.
- **NARRATOR:** People briefed on the surveillance said officials had concluded that Alice Huang was a Chinese intelligence asset.
- **NARRATOR:** It also said Huang never got a defensive briefing. A warning agencies sometimes give.
- **NARRATOR:** Nvidia's answer came fast. Megaspeed was one of many regional customers, it said.
- **NARRATOR:** Huang said the United States government had never told him of its suspicions.
- **NARRATOR:** China's foreign ministry called the spy claim groundless speculation.
- **NARRATOR:** Attorney General Todd Blanche later said the Justice Department had reached no conclusion on whether she was a spy.
- **NARRATOR:** Singapore added a twist. Its Home Affairs ministry said she was never investigated for espionage.
- **NARRATOR:** They found too little evidence to charge her. But they warned her, and banned her from returning, for giving inaccurate information about her role.
- **NARRATOR:** So where does that leave us? A spy? Reported. Not proven. [CARD]
- **NARRATOR:** November fifth. Nvidia's top lawyer reportedly goes to Huang's home. And asks if the affair reports are true.
- **NARRATOR:** He reportedly said they were.
- **LAWYER:** Jensen, are the reports true? (dramatised)
- **HUANG:** They are. (dramatised)
- **NARRATOR:** The same week, three House chairs asked him for a closed-door briefing.
- **NARRATOR:** November eighth. He tells his colleagues. And he does not name her.
- **NARRATOR:** The board opens an independent investigation. It says it has no reason to believe national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised.
- **DIRECTOR:** We need outside lawyers. (dramatised)
- **SECOND DIRECTOR:** Call them tonight. (dramatised)
- **NARRATOR:** The next day, Nvidia stock drops more than five percent. [COUNTER 0->5.0 orange]

- **NARRATOR:** On November twelfth, Nvidia announces that Huang has resigned. Effective immediately.
- **NARRATOR:** But one question will not go away. Officials reportedly knew about her for months. So why, the Times said, was he never warned? [CARD]

## CHAPTER 5 - LESSON (day print) - Look Inside the Box

- **SAVER:** But I never bought Nvidia. (dramatised)
- **ANALYST:** Check your index fund. (dramatised)
- **SAVER:** I own an index fund? (dramatised)
- **ANALYST:** Probably. Open it. (dramatised)
- **NARRATOR:** If you hold an S&P 500 index fund, about six percent of it is Nvidia.
- **NARRATOR:** This spring it was closer to eight and a half. In 2022, it was about one.
- **NARRATOR:** That is roughly six hundred dollars of every ten thousand you invest. [COUNTER 0->600 gold]
- **NARRATOR:** When Nvidia dropped about eleven percent, the rest of the index cushioned it. This time.
- **NARRATOR:** A decade ago, the ten biggest companies were under a fifth of the S&P 500. Today, seven are about a third.
- **NARRATOR:** That is called concentration risk. And one person can move it. [CARD]
- **NARRATOR:** Here are five things worth checking.
- **NARRATOR:** One. How much of your fund is just a few companies?
- **NARRATOR:** Two. Does one founder hold the whole company together?
- **NARRATOR:** Three. Does one customer or partner drive big growth?
- **NARRATOR:** Four. Can the biggest customers be checked? Or are they a mystery?
- **NARRATOR:** Five. Do the board and the rules move before the headlines do?
- **NARRATOR:** This is education, not financial advice. Just a habit: open your fund, and look inside the box.
