# Documentary script (Template A)

One structure for every video: money stories, corruption, scams, rackets, fraud. Default length **10 minutes**
(the owner may ask for another length; scale with section 4). Audience: **US**. Style: a true-crime thriller told by
a calm narrator with short voiced character moments, built to keep viewers watching.

The storytelling *techniques* were studied from a successful channel (see the owner's research notes). Use the
techniques only. **Never copy any creator's script, wording, story order, characters or titles.**

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
## 1. Hard rules (always)

**Truth and fairness**
- Every factual claim is researched (web search is required; if it is unavailable, say so and stop).
- Prefer primary sources: court records, indictments, plea agreements, judgments, DOJ / SEC / FTC / CFTC / FBI /
  state AG press releases, regulator filings (10-K, 8-K), congressional records, company statements. Then major
  outlets (AP, Reuters, NYT, WSJ, Bloomberg, FT, WaPo). Note the date of every number.
- **Proven vs alleged:** a conviction, plea or judgment is stated as fact; charges, lawsuits and reports are
  "charged with", "accused of", "according to". Never call a person guilty without a conviction or plea.
- Real people only as documented. **No invented quotes.** Dialogue moments are dramatised: they may paraphrase what a
  person is documented to have said or done, never put new claims in their mouth. Mark dramatised lines in the
  script file (`"dramatised": true`).
- Private individuals (victims, family members) are not named unless they chose to be public.
- Keep a **"not checked / uncertain"** list and show it to the owner.

**Safety**
- Money content is education, not advice: no buy/sell tips, no promises of returns. Add "not financial advice"
  to the description when money decisions come up.
- Scam and racket stories explain *how people were fooled and how to spot it*, never step-by-step instructions to
  run a scam.

**Craft**
- Spoken American English, short sentences (median 6-8 words, many 3-5). No headings inside the voiceover.
- No "in this video", no "subscribe", no "like", no wrap-up phrases ("to sum up", "in conclusion").
- Original wording every time.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
## 2. Workflow

0. **Read the repo first** (`CLAUDE.md` step 1): the script must match how the engine and past videos work.
1. **Brief.** Ask (one message): *do you already have a script, should I research and write one, or find the idea
   first?* + the topic (or "find a strong story"), the **length (always ask; never assume 10 min)**, anything to avoid.
   If the owner has a script, fact-check it and fit it to Template A; wait for the owner to lock the final script.
   After it is locked, list the new characters, places and props it needs (see `CLAUDE.md` step 3).
2. **Research** (section 3) -> a **fact sheet**: timeline, people (role, proven/alleged), key numbers with source +
   date, quotes that really exist (with source), the money trail, and how it reaches ordinary Americans.
3. **Three outline options.** Same facts, three different angles (e.g. follow the villain / follow the victim /
   follow the investigator). For each: working title, the hook's first 3 lines, the midpoint twist, the wallet line,
   the running motif, the five chapter titles. Recommend one with the reason. The owner picks or mixes.
4. **Full script** in Template A (section 4) with every rule in sections 5-8.
5. **Self-check** with the checklist (section 9); fix before showing.
6. **Deliver** the outputs (section 10).

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
## 3. Research method
- Build the timeline first (dates), then the money trail (who paid whom, how much), then the people.
- Two independent sources for every central fact; one primary source wherever one exists.
- Convert every big number into something an American can feel (section 7). Use official population figures
  (US Census) for "per American"; show the maths in the fact sheet.
- Find the **wallet link**: how the story reaches the viewer's money (taxes, prices, pension / 401(k), bank fees,
  insurance, a fund they own, a product they buy). It must be true and sourced, never forced.
- Find the **midpoint twist** in the facts: the reveal that flips the story (an investigator was already watching,
  the victim was in on it, the money went somewhere unexpected).

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
## 4. Template A (the structure - do not change the order)

At 155 words per minute. Times for 10:00 (speech ends ~9:40); for other lengths keep the same **shares**.

| Time (10 min) | Share | Words | Beat | Must contain |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00-0:45 | 7.5% | ~115 | **HOOK** | (1) start in the middle of the most striking scene; (2) the shock line first and it confirms the title/thumbnail in the first 5 s; (3) dramatic irony (the viewer learns what someone in the story doesn't know); (4) a stakes number by 0:20; (5) an **early wallet tease** (one line); (6) one open question; (7) a promise of where it starts. Then the **title card**. |
| 0:45-2:30 | 17.5% | ~270 | **CH 1 - ORIGIN** | Re-hook first line. Who the main person/organisation is and why they matter; one personal detail; plant a set-up that pays off later. Ends on a cliffhanger. |
| 2:30-4:15 | 17.5% | ~270 | **CH 2 - THE OPENING** | The problem or the opportunity (the "locked door"). Pay off one hook loop, open a new one. One analogy scene. Cliffhanger. |
| 4:15-5:15 | 10% | ~155 | **CH 3 - THE PLAN** | How the scheme / plan worked, step by step in plain words, with one A-vs-B or everyday analogy. Cliffhanger. |
| 5:15-6:00 | 7.5% | ~115 | **MIDPOINT TWIST** | The biggest reveal of the video ("What nobody knew..."). On a text card. Music drops. |
| 6:00-7:45 | 17.5% | ~270 | **CH 4 - THE FALLOUT** | Consequences: investigations, arrests, collapse, denials. Sources said out loud. Proven vs alleged stated carefully. Cliffhanger. |
| 7:45-9:00 | 12.5% | ~195 | **CH 5 - YOUR WALLET** | "Here's where this reaches your money": the true, sourced link + one per-American number + one practical takeaway (how to spot it / what to watch). |
| 9:00-9:40 | 7% | ~105 | **CALLBACK ENDING** | Return to the opening scene / image; pay off the last loop; end on an open question on a black card. The end screen plays over this final shot. |

Total ~1,500 words for 10 minutes.

**Chapter titles:** 2-5 words, intriguing, never descriptive ("The Second Key", "Paper Profits", "The Quiet Phone",
"Nobody Counted", "The Empty Office"). Shown on a chapter card. Never reuse another creator's titles.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
## 5. Retention rules (apply everywhere)
1. **Something new every 20-30 s:** a text card, a dialogue moment, a counter, a stamp, an analogy, a time/place jump.
2. **Loop stacking:** at least 2 open questions are always alive; close one only after opening another.
3. **Re-hook** at the start of every chapter (restate the stakes or the open question in one line).
4. **Cliffhanger** as the last line before every chapter card (a short line that makes the next chapter necessary).
5. **Set-up -> payoff:** at least 3 planted details pay off later ("Later, someone will.").
6. **Running motif:** one repeated phrase, object or countdown word, 3-5 times, last time in the ending.
7. **Turn phrases** (vary them, don't overuse): "But here's the strange part." "What nobody knew..." "And here's where
   this reaches your money." "That's his problem. Here's yours."
8. **Comedy breaks tension:** a short dry joke or absurd true detail about every 90 s.
9. **No ending signals** before the last 30 s.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
## 6. Dialogue moments
- About a quarter of all lines; each moment <= 10 s, 2-4 lines, two speakers.
- Turns a fact into a scene; usually ends on a joke or a sting line.
- Real people: dramatised and consistent with the record (rule 1). Generic roles (officer, accountant, banker,
  investor, clerk) can speak freely.

## 7. Numbers
- At most one big number per 30 s. Every big number gets: a source + date, a relatable conversion ("about $1,700 for
  every American", "a new car for every...", "$650 of every $10,000 in your fund"), and a **counter** in the script
  file (from, to, unit, colour: gold = money, orange = loss).
- Round for speech ("about $4.8 billion"); keep the exact figure in the fact sheet.

## 8. Text cards
- 15-20 per 10 minutes: the strongest sentence of a section, typed word by word on screen.
- Always: the shock line, the irony line, the midpoint twist, the final question. Max 12 words per card.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
## 9. Self-check before showing (all must be true)
- [ ] Hook <= 45 s, title card at ~0:45; first line confirms the title; wallet tease present.
- [ ] 5 chapters + midpoint twist + callback ending, in order; times within 10% of the table.
- [ ] Every chapter starts with a re-hook and ends with a cliffhanger.
- [ ] >= 2 open loops at all times until the ending; >= 3 set-up/payoffs; motif used 3-5 times.
- [ ] Something new every 20-30 s (check the line list).
- [ ] Every number sourced, dated, converted; proven vs alleged correct; no invented quotes; private people unnamed.
- [ ] No "subscribe", no "in this video", no wrap-up phrases; original wording.
- [ ] Word count = 155 x speech minutes (±5%).

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
## 10. Outputs
1. **`script.md`** (for the owner): title + 3 alternatives, the outline, the full script with beat headers, speaker
   labels, `[CARD]` marks on text-card lines, `[COUNTER from->to]` marks, chapter titles; word count and length.
2. **`script.json`** (for the animation step): one entry per line:
   `{"id": "n014", "beat": "CH2", "chapter": "The Second Key", "speaker": "NARRATOR", "text": "...",
     "card": false, "counter": {"from": 0, "to": 4800000000, "unit": "$", "colour": "orange"},
     "dramatised": false, "sources": ["S3"], "shot_hint": "top-down desk, ledger slides in"}`
3. **`voice_script.md`**: lines grouped by speaker, with delivery notes, for the owner's voice tool.
4. **`sources.md`**: numbered sources (S1...) with title, publisher, date, URL, and what each supports; plus the
   "not checked / uncertain" list.
5. **YouTube chapters** (timestamps from the template) and a description draft with sources.

Then tell the owner plainly: the length, the angle chosen, which facts are uncertain, and which lines are dramatised.
